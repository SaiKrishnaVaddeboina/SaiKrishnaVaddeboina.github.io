The Problem

Mobile ticketing opened a fraud loophole that count-based rules can't see.

When a rider activates their MBTA ticket only after spotting an inspector — compressing purchase, activation, and barcode scan into seconds — the inspector sees a valid ticket, the system logs a compliant rider, and the agency takes an invisible revenue hit.

Masabi's existing Pattern rule engine flags riders with three or more sub-15s activation-scan gaps in a 10-day window (Rule A) or sub-30s in 7 days (Rule B). The rules are interpretable and operationally deployed — but by design, they're blind to three classes of evasion:

  • Purchase compression — buying, activating, and scanning all within seconds upon inspector approach.
  • Sequence alternation — fast-then-slow patterns that stay below the three-event threshold in any rolling window.
  • Inspector reactivity — riders who activate only when an inspector is physically present.
The Approach

A four-layer pipeline that extends — not replaces — the existing rule engine.

01

Pattern Rule Engine (Baseline)

Existing rule engine retained as descriptive baseline, flagging 58,752 riders (26.5%). Rule flags carry 20% weight in the final composite score — hybrid by design, not replacement.

02

9-State Hidden Markov Model

Categorical HMM trained over 7-symbol behavioral vocabulary. State count selected by Bayesian Information Criterion across 70 Baum-Welch fits over {2,3,4,5,7,9,11} states. Output: smooth posterior dominance scores per rider.

03

Composite Anomaly Score

Linear combination of four normalized signals: inspector reactivity (30%), HMM posterior (25%), gaming suspicion (25%), rule confirmation (20%). Continuous 0–1 risk score replaces binary flags.

04

K-Means Operational Clustering

K=6 selected via silhouette analysis (peak 0.183). Segments the 61,869 anomaly-selected riders into operationally distinct enforcement archetypes — each mapped to a specific intervention.

Feature Engineering

86 features across 14 logical groups.

Built a rider-level feature table of dimension 221,382 × 87, spanning timing, volume, personal-baseline deviation, line concentration, threshold-gaming ratios, inspector reactivity, time-of-day, geographic spread, sequence entropy, HMM dwell, and weather context.

Behavioral Vocabulary

Discretized continuous activation-scan timing into 7 mutually exclusive symbols, including PURCHASE_THEN_ACTIVATE_FAST and GAMING_THRESHOLD — symbols Pattern's rule engine has no concept of.

Inspector Presence Proxy

Inferred at vehicle_id × 1-minute granularity by aggregating handheld-scan density. Top 5% of buckets flagged as high-presence; per-rider inspector_reactivity_gap hits its theoretical max of 1.0 in the top 20 most reactive riders.

Gaming Suspicion Score

Combines three timing hot-zone ratios (13–15s, 16–18s, 25–30s) with double weight on the 16–18s band — the most operationally costly "just above Rule A" zone.

Weather Enrichment

Hourly Open-Meteo data joined for July–December 2025 to test whether evasion rate covaries with weather. Treated as cluster-profiling context, not a primary score signal.

The HMM Finding

Three habitual evasion archetypes — self-transitions above 0.97.

The fitted 9-state HMM identifies three latent states with self-transition probabilities exceeding 0.97 — direct empirical evidence that fare evasion is habitual, not situational. Once a rider enters an evasion state, they stay there.

Engineering Decisions That Mattered

Four sponsor-mandated adjustments shaped the pipeline.

  • joblib parallelization — Baum-Welch fits across 8 cores via the loky backend. The standard multiprocessing.spawn backend hangs on the Windows training environment.
  • FIFO observation cap — limited to 30 events per rider, reducing 1.9M observations to 1.1M while preserving the most recent rider behavior and avoiding numerical underflow on long sequences.
  • Stratified 50/50 sampling — 65,376 training riders balanced between rule-flagged and unflagged populations, with a 5-event minimum threshold for inclusion.
  • Posterior dominance over Viterbi — replaced the bimodal Viterbi-based scoring with smooth predict_proba() output, giving a continuous distribution that's actually operationally usable.
What's Next

From unsupervised pipeline → supervised system.

The unsupervised approach was deliberate — labels weren't available at inception. With the 60-rider Masabi review sheet returned with EVADER / NON-EVADER labels, the next phase pivots to:

  • Positive-Unlabeled (PU) learning using rule flags as conservative positive seeds.
  • Snorkel-style weak supervision with five labeling functions to scale training data beyond the human-validated subset.
  • A new Pattern rule for inspector-reactive activators — closing one structural blind spot without requiring full pipeline deployment.
  • Cross-agency generalization to other Masabi customer agencies on the Justride platform.
